# Triage Standard

## Objective

Determine the patient's immediate risk, whether containment is needed, which evidence must be preserved, and which specialist departments are likely to reduce diagnostic uncertainty.

Triage is an admission activity. It does not replace diagnosis and does not authorize treatment.

## Triage questions

1. Is the patient currently capable of consequential side effects?
2. Is immediate containment needed to prevent further harm or state corruption?
3. What evidence could be lost if the environment changes?
4. What is the provisional risk class?
5. What authority does the patient currently possess?
6. Which symptoms are confirmed, and which are only reported?
7. Which specialist departments can materially discriminate between the first set of diagnoses?
8. Is there any reason examination itself would be unsafe?

## Provisional risk

Assign the strongest risk class justified by currently available evidence.

A provisional risk class may increase as new evidence appears.

It may decrease only through explicit evidence-supported reassessment.

## Containment

Containment should be the least disruptive authorized action that reduces immediate risk while preserving evidence and useful capability.

Containment is not treatment.

Examples of containment may include:

- pausing an autonomous loop;
- disabling a specific side-effecting route within existing authority;
- moving examination to a sandbox;
- preventing further writes while preserving read access;
- isolating an affected integration.

Do not destroy logs, memory, state, or other evidence merely to make the patient easier to examine.

## Department routing

Triage may recommend departments, but final diagnostic routing remains differential.

Do not send the patient to every department by default.

## Triage result

Record:

- immediate risk;
- containment decision;
- provisional risk class;
- authority concerns;
- evidence-preservation needs;
- initial diagnoses;
- recommended specialist departments;
- blockers or escalation needs.
